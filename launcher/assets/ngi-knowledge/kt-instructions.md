# kT-RAM instructions and conductance
kT-RAM represents a signed synapse using a differential memristor pair. Conductance evolves under applied drive; emulator values are simulated, not physical hardware measurements.
FF is forward floating read, not a dedicated write or feedback instruction. It computes a readout from conductances under an applied read voltage. In the installed emulator, evaluation first obtains the readout, then applies the corresponding drive to active synapses. That read drive can also adapt conductances: a read need not be non-disturbing. This possible read-induced adaptation does not reclassify FF as a write instruction. Adaptation depends on drive, device model, and operating conditions; a sub-threshold read can leave conductances unchanged.
The Gateway's Read State button instead calls snapshot-only GET /api/state, without evaluating FF or applying a drive. RF is reverse floating read. Repeated FF can have anti-Hebbian behavior; RF can have Hebbian behavior. Calling FF a read describes its readout operation, not a guarantee of unchanged state.
Installed Relay accepts FF, FH, FL, FU, FA, FZ, RF, RH, RL, RU, RA, RZ, FFLV, and RFLV. Floating, high, low, unsupervised, anti-unsupervised, zero, and low-voltage variants are distinct operations. Backend instruction validation and the installed emulator are authoritative. Do not guess unsupported voltage controls or arbitrary numeric-input endpoints.
Conductances ga and gb describe the two sides; magnitude is their sum. These are separate from RWKV state, numeric mapping, and evaluation noise. A draft instruction proposal does not execute an instruction.
